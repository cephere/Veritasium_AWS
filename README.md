  <h3>Abstract</h3>

  <p align="justify">
    Advanced technologies such as Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning are regarded as significant milestones 
    in the fight against the dissemination of fake news (Janiesch, Zschech & Heinrich, 2021). This study introduces 
    Veritasium, a benchmarking system designed for fake news detection, with the primary objective of automatically 
    determining whether information disseminated online is real or fake. Utilizing supervised learning, a dataset 
    comprising both real and fake news was employed to train the algorithms. The study found that the Logistic Regression,
    Support Vector Classifier, and Passive Aggressive Classifier algorithms exhibited the highest training and testing accuracy,
    using a normalized pixel size of 250 x 250. Web scrapers were developed using BeautifulSoup to gather image and text data 
    from news articles. The trained algorithms were then combined into an ensemble model, which integrated the three selected 
    algorithms to produce a single final prediction. When evaluated with a validated dataset, the model achieved an accuracy of 
    62%, and 67.5% when incorporating input from researchers. Additionally, a comparison model was tested, achieving an accuracy 
    of 63%, similar to that of Veritasium. This supports the accuracy of the model and indicates that the model developed in this 
    study performs better than random guessing; however, the accuracy remains moderate rather than exceptionally high. The findings 
    demonstrate the feasibility of a benchmarking system for fake news detection using supervised learning and suggest potential for 
    its integration into platforms as a means of combating the spread of misinformation.
